# Water Rocket Tower

`data/towers/water_rocket.json` defines its cost, range, cooldown, damage, flight speed, turn speed, lifetime, and three upgrades. The placement scene adds it as the fourth tower tile. Its shots use the existing targeting and cooldown code in `placement_tower_attack.gd`; damage is applied only when the rocket reaches its target.

The tower uses `art/blender/towers/water_rocket_tower.blend`. The `water_rocket_tower_mk1` collection exports the tower and `rocket` exports the shared parked/flying rocket. `scenes/water_rocket_visual.tscn` scales the tower to 70% to fit a tile. `Base/pivot` turns the plate and launcher; the separate `base_mesh` and pump stay stationary. The imported `pitch` empty under the pivot rotates the right arm. The Blender model's right side points along Blender +Y (Godot -Z), while its front points along Godot -X, so the aiming code applies a quarter-turn to face the front toward the enemy. Blender Y rotation maps to Godot -Z; `scripts/water_rocket_visual.gd` rotates the imported pivot around Godot Z to aim the rocket forward. The elevation changes from 45 degrees above horizontal for a nearby target to 20 degrees at maximum attack range. The rocket launches along the pitched muzzle and homes in flight.

`pump_scaler` sits at the bottom of the pump. Blender's local Z becomes Godot's local Y, so the firing Tween scales its Y axis to compress the pump, launches at the bottom, then expands and settles. The loaded rocket is attached to the imported `muzzle` empty. The rocket collection's `rocket_base` is at the same Blender position as the muzzle; `scenes/water_rocket_mesh.tscn` offsets the exported model so this point becomes its origin. `rocket_tip` is a useful reference for the nose, but the current projectile only needs the model to point along local +Y. Both parked and flying rockets use the same scene and 70% scale.

`scenes/water_rocket_projectile.tscn` owns the independent flying rocket. Each physics step turns its direction toward the target by a limited angle, then moves using `projectile_speed * delta`. The configured `homing_turn_speed` shapes the launch arc; after the first quarter second, the turn limit rises as the rocket nears its target so it can complete the final approach without circling. If its target is defeated, freed, or reaches the objective, the rocket spirals upward and ends in a visual water firework and spout without dealing damage. The spiral has its own short duration so it finishes even when the normal projectile lifetime is nearly over. A segment check catches hits even when a frame moves farther than the impact radius.

The projectile's `WaterTrail` uses world-space `GPUParticles3D` with 0.6 second cyan droplets, downward gravity, and small size variation. `placement_shot_effects.gd` creates a separate 0.28 second `WaterNozzleSpray` burst at launch. Tune amount, lifetime, velocity, spread, gravity, and droplet size in those scripts; no texture files are needed.

After editing the Blender source, export both collections with `python tools/export_models.py export --kind tower --id water_rocket`, then reimport the GLBs in Godot. Preserve the named hierarchy and the matching `muzzle` and `rocket_base` positions. If the rocket base moves in Blender, update the offset in `water_rocket_mesh.tscn` as well.

Open `scenes/tower_placement.tscn`, select the fourth tile, place the tower near a route, and start a wave to test it. The water rocket test script checks the launch sequence, homing, damage, and upgrades.
